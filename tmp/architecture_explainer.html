Modular Separation of Concerns (Progressive Disclosure)

The LTTD system is structured into **6 strict architecture layers**. Each layer has a unique, unidirectional responsibility flowing from Layer 1 down to Layer 6. This prevents statistical feedback loops, lookahead contamination, and ensures modular testability.

Layer 1: Regime Detection

src/regime/

Infers market state (BULL, BEAR, or SIDEWAYS) using a 3-state Hidden Markov Model (HMM) fitted on daily log returns and realized volatility. Has **zero dependency** on price indicators to prevent circular logic.

Layer 2: Signal Engine

src/signals/

Calculates individual directional signals ∈ {-1, +1} using a strictly causal base filter framework. Indicators include technical indicators (Kalman RSI, Fourier Supertrend, TSI) and live-fetched on-chain metrics (STH-MVRV, STH-SOPR, NUPL, STH Supply in Profit).

Layer 3: Feature Processing

src/features/

Eliminates multicollinearity. Performs standardisation, **VIF (Variance Inflation Factor) pruning** (dropping variables with VIF > 10), and **PCA orthogonalization** to extract orthogonal components capturing >85% of feature variance.

Layer 4: Ensemble Aggregation

src/ensemble/

Aggregates features into a consolidated Final Score ∈ [-1.0, +1.0] using walk-forward optimized (WFO) classifiers like L1-regularized Logistic Regression (Lasso) or XGBoost.

Layer 5: Execution Engine

src/execution/

Translates the final score and regime state into a target exposure (strictly binary 0.0 or 1.0) using asymmetric Ehlers SuperSmoother, MHP, RCO, Circuit Breaker, and the 150-day moving average filter. Results are persisted to SQLite.

Layer 6: Presentation

backend/ + frontend/

Serves persisted SQLite data via Hono (port :8765) and visualizes the real-time execution dashboard on a React/Vite web application (port :8766).

Anatomy of Whipsaws in Crypto Bear Markets

Bitcoin bear markets (e.g. 2018, 2022) are characterized by long-term structural downward trends interspersed with sudden, high-velocity **"relief rallies"**. These rallies show strong short-term upward momentum, causing standard technical indicators (RSI, Supertrend, etc.) and model final scores to spike.

If a trend-following system acts immediately on these short-term scores, it triggers a buy entry. However, because the macro regime remains bearish, the rally quickly exhausts, the price rolls over, and the system is forced to sell at a loss.

The Downside Impact

  • Increased Churn: High frequency of buying high and selling low.
  • Trading Friction: Heavy transaction fees and slippage eating capital.
  • Emotional/System Stress: Model confidence jumps in fakeouts, leading to localized drawdowns.

The "Zero-Lag" Illusion: Savitzky-Golay Lookahead Bias

In the legacy Pine Script (`0xbujang-lttd.pinescript`), Indicator 12 (Savitzky-Golay Flow Bands) was used to smooth scores. It appeared to have zero lag and gave incredible backtest results.

However, audit analysis discovered a fatal **lookahead bias**. The mathematical coefficients were coded in a way that peeked at future price points (symmetric centering window). In a backtester, it fits the curve perfectly because the future is known. In live execution, it is mathematically impossible to replicate, causing disastrous performance.

Causal Requirement Check

Every indicator in LTTD must pass a strict `test_no_lookahead()` check. Signals computed at time $t$ must depend **strictly** on indexes $\le t$. John Ehlers' SuperSmoother is a fully causal time-domain filter that honors this rule.

SuperSmoother Equation Deep Dive

Designed by John Ehlers, the 2-Pole SuperSmoother matches a second-order analog Butterworth filter. Its difference equation is:

# Coefficients for cutoff period P
a1 = exp(-1.414 * pi / P)
b1 = 2 * a1 * cos(1.414 * pi / P)
c2 = b1
c3 = -a1 * a1
c1 = 1.0 - c2 - c3
# Recursive Filter Difference Eq:
y[t] = c1 * (x[t] + x[t-1])/2.0 + c2 * y[t-1] + c3 * y[t-2]

Because it utilizes recursive history components ($y[t-1], y[t-2]$), it exhibits steep, near-brickwall filtering characteristics with significantly less group delay compared to exponential moving averages.

150-day MA Trend Filter Logic

While Ehlers SuperSmoother acts as a **local** noise filter on the *score* domain, the 150-day Simple Moving Average acts as a **global** regime validator in the *price* domain. Entries are only validated if the asset close is above the 150-day MA, avoiding catching falling knives in bear years.

Audit of Complaints & Solutions

How Ehlers SuperSmoother, MHP, RCO, and disabled BEAR override resolve specific noise issues:

2019-04-09 Breakout RESOLVED

Complaint: Whipsaw exit triggered during the major April breakout.

Resolution: The 12-day MHP locked exposure at 1.0. Turning off `USE_BEAR_OVERRIDE` prevented single-day regime switches from forcing exits.

2020-02-16 to 2020-03-03 RESOLVED

Complaint: Sold too early during a mid-trend correction.

Resolution: The SuperSmoother and MHP held exposure at 1.0 through February 29th, exiting only in March once the breakdown was confirmed.

2020-05-23 Sideways RESOLVED

Complaint: Exit triggered during post-halving consolidations.

Resolution: Ehlers SuperSmoother smoothed the horizontal score fluctuations, preventing exit thresholds from being breached.

2022-04-08 to 2022-04-15 RESOLVED

Complaint: Noise trade triggered in the middle of a bear market dump.

Resolution: Stayed completely OUT (exposure = 0.0) as the price was below the 150-day MA filter, and the valuation circuit breaker was active.
