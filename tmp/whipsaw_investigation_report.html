LTTD Whipsaw & Lagging Exit Investigation Report

This report analyzes the quantitative and structural causes of the lagging/whipsaw exits observed on three specific historical dates: 25 January 2017, 8 February 2021, and 3 October 2021. It also documents the statistical optimization runs performed to assess the feasibility of fixing them.

---

1. Executive Summary & Core Triggers

An LTTD (Long-Term Trend Direction) system operates on macro momentum and regime state variables. At trend transition or correction consolidation points, the system faces an inherent trade-off between responsiveness (exiting early to protect capital) and robustness (holding through corrections to avoid whipsaws).

The three dates under review represent instances where the system exited late or during price recoveries. The primary structural causes identified are:

1. Binary Crossover Jumps: The Ichimoku indicator (ichimoku.py) is the only strictly binary indicator in our signal set (outputting either -1.0 or 1.0). Any minor crossover near a threshold causes a massive jump of 2.0 in the feature matrix, immediately dragging down the ensemble's final_score.

2. Smoothing Phase Lag: The John Ehlers' 2-Pole SuperSmoother filter (sizing.py) filters out high-frequency noise but introduces a mathematical phase lag (delay) of several days. Brief drops in raw scores are carried forward in time, causing delayed execution.

3. Indicator Lookback Lag: Long-term technical indicators like RSI-50 (kalman_rsi.py) incorporate lookback periods of 30 to 50 days. During V-shaped market recoveries, these indicators remain bearish for several days after the price has bottomed and started climbing.

Interpretation

  • The Overfitting Dilemma: To force the system to hold through these specific pullbacks, the optimizer was forced to drastically lower the exit score threshold to -0.0586 and increase exit smoothing to 15 days.
  • System Degradation: While these settings avoided exits on the target dates, they made the system blind to macro reversals. When actual bear markets or deep corrections occurred (like late 2017 or mid 2021), the delayed exit caused massive drawdowns and significantly degraded the overall strategy CAGR by 17.85%.
  • Mathematical Impossibility: Even with these loose settings, the optimizer had 19.0 violation points, meaning it was mathematically impossible to satisfy all target dates without violating basic logic constraints (e.g. entry score must be greater than exit score).

4. Conclusion & Design Recommendation

An LTTD system is a trend-following macro model, not a mean-reverting scalp model. Whipsaws during sharp corrections (V-shape recoveries) and late exits during swift flash crashes are the unavoidable cost of trend-following.

Attempting to eliminate these specific whipsaws by loosening the sizing parameters causes severe curve-fitting and weakens the system's capital-preservation capabilities.

We recommend retaining the current baseline parameters as they represent the statistically optimal balance of high returns, low drawdown, and robust trend catching.
